## 摘要
我们研究一种固定特征求解器，称为 Spectral-ELM，其中将 Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto（CGL）微分矩阵应用于极限学习机（ELM）试探函数的节点值。曲区域采用映射多片格式处理。每个曲四边形片被表示为参考正方形的像，物理导数由相关度量项计算，相邻片通过强制解及其法向通量的连续性进行耦合。局部 QR 正交化用于减少离散特征之间的近似线性相关。数值研究包括与直接 CGL 配点法、解析微分的 ELM、全局随机特征方法以及 TransNet 采样策略的比较。还包括一个匹配的 KdV 测试、一个小型三维示例，以及一个含孔曲区域上的泊松问题。对于正方形上的光滑椭圆问题，直接 CGL 配点法给出最小误差。当应用于相同的固定特征试探空间时，谱微分与解析微分产生相当的误差。对于曲区域问题，映射直接 CGL 配点法和映射 Spectral-ELM 在未知量数量相当的情况下达到相似的精度。当前格式特别适用于一至三维的光滑偏微分方程，其中张量积网格可实现精确的高阶离散，而稀疏网格和维数自适应策略为扩展到更高维问题提供了有前景的方向。

## Abstract
We study a fixed-feature solver, referred to as Spectral-ELM, in which Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto (CGL) differentiation matrices are applied to the nodal values of an extreme learning machine (ELM) trial function. Curved domains are treated using a mapped multi-patch formulation. Each curved quadrilateral patch is represented as the image of a reference square, physical derivatives are computed from the associated metric terms, and adjacent patches are coupled by enforcing continuity of the solution and its normal flux. Local QR orthogonalization is used to reduce near-linear dependence among the discrete features. The numerical study includes comparisons with direct CGL collocation, an analytically differentiated ELM, a global Random Feature Method, and a TransNet sampling strategy. It also includes a matched KdV test, a small three-dimensional example, and a Poisson problem on a curved domain containing a hole. For a smooth elliptic problem on a square, direct CGL collocation gives the smallest error. When applied to the same fixed-feature trial space, spectral and analytic differentiation produce comparable errors. For the curved-domain problem, mapped direct CGL collocation and mapped Spectral-ELM achieve similar accuracy with comparable numbers of unknowns. The present formulation is particularly well suited to smooth PDEs in one to three dimensions, where tensor-product grids enable accurate high-order discretizations, while sparse-grid and dimension-adaptive strategies offer promising directions for extension to higher-dimensional problems.
