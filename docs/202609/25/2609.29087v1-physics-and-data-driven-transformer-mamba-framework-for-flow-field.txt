ABSTRACT 

While deep learning accelerates expensive partial differential equation solving in computational fluid dynamics (CFD), exist-ing methods like PINNs and FNOs often struggle with general-ization, noise robustness, and physical consistency. We intro-duce the Transformer-Mamba for Flow Field (TM4FF) frame-work, a physics-constrained operator learning model with three key innovations: a Residual Wavelet Mamba (RWM) layer for feature denoising, a Transformer-based attention mechanism for enhanced feature fusion, and a physics-informed loss us-ing Fourier derivatives to enforce the Navier-Stokes equations. Experiments on four CFD datasets show TM4FF achieves high accuracy and robust generalization across varying flow condi-tions. 

Index Terms — Multimodal Fusion, Deep Learning, State Space Models, fluid dynamics, AI for Science 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Solving partial differential equations (PDEs) in computational fluid dynamics (CFD) with traditional numerical methods [1– 3] is computationally intensive. Deep learning offers a promis-ing alternative to accelerate simulations by orders of magni-tude [4, 5], with some models already surpassing traditional methods in accuracy for certain problems [6–10]. Two dominant deep learning paradigms have emerged to tackle these problems. Physics-Informed Neural Net-works (PINNs) [11–13] integrate physical laws as constraints but often struggle to generalize across varying flow condi-tions [14–16]. In response, data-driven operator learning methods, such as Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) [17] and Deep Operator Networks (DeepONets) [18], were developed to learn mappings between function spaces, offering better gen-eralization. While these models, including architectures like U-Net [19], successfully capture spatial hierarchies, several challenges remain: 

W1:) Difficulty in modeling the complex temporal depen-dencies and multi-scale correlations present in unsteady flow fields.

W2:) Poor generalization to unseen PDE parameters, phys-ical properties, domain geometry, etc.), requiring retraining as parameters change. 

W3:) Current data-driven methods lack explicit physical constraints. Although networks learn certain physical laws through training, these are often implicit and may not guaran-tee adherence to fundamental physical principles. 

W4:) Noise generated during training (e.g., striping noise) affects the accuracy of specific flow field predictions. To address these limitations, we propose the Transformer-Mamba for Flow Field (TM4FF) framework. TM4FF treats fluid flow as a spatiotemporal image sequence and enhances generalization by directly embedding operational parameters into the neural network. We introduce several architectural innovations: a novel Residual Wavelet Mamba (RWM) Layer uses a dual-branch structure with Haar DWT to decouple and filter noise (addressing W4 ). We replace traditional skip con-nections with a Transformer-based self-attention mechanism to intelligently fuse multi-scale features (addressing W2 ). Finally, we incorporate a physics-informed loss function with Fourier derivatives to enforce the Navier-Stokes equations, ensuring the model’s predictions are physically consistent (addressing 

W3 ), while the Mamba architecture itself excels at capturing long-range spatiotemporal dependencies (addressing W1 ). As shown in Fig. 1, comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance. Our main contributions are: • TM4FF Framework: A novel architecture that embeds op-erational parameters and uses a Mamba encoder to capture spatiotemporal characteristics of unsteady flow. • Residual Wavelet Mamba Layer: A dual-branch layer for noise decoupling, combining a model-driven Haar DWT branch with a data-driven residual branch. • Transformer-based Skip Connection: An intelligent fea-ture fusion mechanism that replaces standard skip connec-tions to enhance information flow between the encoder and decoder. • Physics-Informed Loss with Fourier Derivatives: A hy-brid loss function that constrains the data-driven model with the Navier-Stokes equations for improved accuracy and physical realism. arXiv:2609.29087v1 [cs.LG] 24 Sep 2026 Fig. 1 . 2. METHODOLOGY 2.1. Overall Framework and Condition Embedding 

We aim to approximate the flow field in a domain D =

{(x, y, t )}. The TM4FF framework (Fig. 2(A)) learns an operator G : (Σ , Ω) 7 → u, where Ω represents operational parameters (e.g., boundary conditions, viscosity), Σ is the ve-locity field at time t − ∆t, and u is the field at time t. The network fθ approximates this operator, with optimal parame-ters θ∗ found by minimizing a loss function L(u, ˆu).To make the model aware of varying conditions, opera-tional parameters Ω are embedded. Scalar parameters are tiled to match the spatial resolution of the input flow field u and con-catenated into a tensor x. Global pooling and a fully connected (FC) layer on x produce a feature vector h:

h = FC (concat (Globalmaxpool (x), Globalavgpool (x))) (1) A Sigmoid function generates attention weights a = σ(h),which modulate the input flow field: uin = a ⊙ u. This allows the model to adaptively respond to the given physical condi-tions. 

2.2. Residual Wavelet Mamba Encoder 

The TM4FF encoder (Fig. 2(B)) uses blocks containing our novel Residual Wavelet Mamba (RWM) layer (Fig. 3) for feature extraction and noise decoupling. The RWM layer processes an input feature map S ∈ RC×H×W using a dual-branch structure. A model-driven branch first applies a 2D Haar Wavelet Transform (DWT) to decompose S into four sub-bands (as shown in Fig. 4): 

Sa = λa ∗ S, a ∈ { ll, lh, hl, hh } (2) where λa are Haar filters. This isolates features by fre-quency, helping to filter structured noise. These sub-bands are then processed and fused with features from a parallel data-driven residual branch. 2.3. Bi-level Skip Connection (BiS) 

We replace standard skip connections with a Bi-level skip connection (BiS) to improve encoder-decoder feature fusion. This module employs Bi-level Routing Attention ((as shown in Fig. 5)) [21], an sparse attention mechanism. For a feature map divided into regions, it computes an adjacency matrix Ar be-tween region-level queries and keys. Instead of dense attention, it finds the top-k most relevant regions for each query: 

Ir = topkIndex( Ar ) (6) Attention is then computed only over these selected regions. The final output combines this sparse attention with a Local Context Enhancement (LCE) operation [22]: 

O = Attention( Q, Kg , Vg ) + LCE( V) (7) Fig. 4 . The wavelet decomposition and reconstruction of strip features in neural networks (DWT aggregates strip noise into

2.4. Physics-Informed Loss Function 

To ensure physical plausibility, we constrain the model with the incompressible Navier-Stokes (N-S) equations via a hybrid loss function. The total loss L = Ldata + Lincompressible + LNS 

is a weighted sum of the standard MSE data loss ( Ldata ) and two physics-based penalties. These penalties are computed ef-ficiently using Fourier Derivatives and consist of the incom-pressibility loss, which enforces the divergence-free condition 

Lincompressible = ∥∇ · u∥2, and the Navier-Stokes residual loss, which penalizes deviations from the momentum equation: 

LNS = ∂u

3. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 3.1. Experimental Setup 

We benchmarked our method on four diverse fluid dynamics datasets from the CFDBench [23]: lid-driven cavity, circular tube, breaking dam, and flow around a cylinder. The data was split into 8:1:1 training, validation, and test sets, ensuring dis-joint operating parameters between splits. All models were trained for 100 epochs on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090, with a learning rate decay factor of 0.9 applied every 20 epochs.

4. CONCLUSION 

We propose the Transformer-Mamba for Flow Field frame-work, a model that enhances computational fluid dynamics prediction by embedding operational parameters. Its key inno-vations include a Residual Wavelet Mamba layer for feature de-noising, a Transformer-based attention mechanism to replace skip connections, and a physics-informed loss with Fourier derivatives to enforce Navier-Stokes equation constraints. Ex-perimental results show that TM4FF achieves high accuracy and generalization across varying flow conditions, providing a promising alternative to traditional CFD methods. 5. REFERENCES
