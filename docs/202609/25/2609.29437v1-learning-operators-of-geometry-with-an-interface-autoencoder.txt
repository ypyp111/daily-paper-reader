# Learning Operators of Geometry with an Interface Autoencoder 

Aiqing Zhu ∗ Pengzhan Jin †

Abstract 

Geometry-dependent PDEs define operators whose inputs and outputs may each consist of an oriented interface and a function on that interface, whereas most existing neural operators are formulated on fixed domains. In this paper, we first establish an approximation theorem for continuous operators between general state sets. We then instantiate the theorem with the Interface Autoencoder (IAE), which represents interface–function states in a fixed box using finite projection codes and decodes them by zero-set extraction and restriction. For tensor cosine codes, we prove a uniform C0 reconstruction rate and tube-local gradient convergence. These estimates yield Hausdorff and graph-Hausdorff error bounds, together with a reference-based topology certificate. The IAE accommodates both cross-space geometric operators, mapping domain geometries to the associated PDE solution fields, and within-space geometric operators, mapping between interface–function states. Its effectiveness is demonstrated through numerical experiments on the Poisson equation, interface merging in Hele–Shaw flow, and two-phase Stokes flow with surfactant. 

Keywords: Operator learning, geometry-dependent PDEs, interface problems, autoencoders, topology-preserving decoding. 

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R37, 41A25, 41A65, 68T07. 

# 1 Introduction 

Many scientific-computing tasks require repeated solutions of partial differential equations indexed by coefficients, data, or geometry. Operator learning addresses this many-query setting by approximating solution maps, with existing methods largely developed for functions on fixed domains and supported by universal approximation theory [4, 11, 12, 16, 19, 20, 23, 28]. However, many problems of interest are geometry-dependent. Examples include structural shape and topology optimization [1, 3], and fluid–structure interaction [6, 34]. Evolving geometries also arise in phase-change problems [17, 40], Hele–Shaw flows [14, 35], multiphase flows with surfactant [39, 43], and PDEs posed on evolving surfaces [8, 9]. In these settings, the domain or interface may itself be an input or output, so the states of the target solution operators may therefore not belong to a common fixed function space. We distinguish two cases in this paper: cross-space operators map domain geometries to the corresponding PDE solution fields, whereas within-space operators map one geometric state to another. In either case, a geometric state may consist of an interface together with a function defined on it. Existing approaches handle the variable-domain setting either by transporting data to a fixed representation space or by encoding geometry in a common domain. Geo-FNO and DIMON use learned or prescribed coordinate maps [24, 45]; D2D and D2E use pullback or extension

If the transport assumes a homeomorphic or diffeomorphic correspondence with a single reference domain, it cannot persist through topology changes. Ambient-domain models instead encode geometric information directly, for example through a domain indicator in DAFNO [26], signed-distance and point-cloud data in GINO [25] and Geom-DeepONet [13], point-cloud integration in PCNO [46], physics-attention in Transolver [29, 41, 47], or coordinate-based neural fields in CORAL [36] and discretization-independent surrogates [7]. In these settings, however, the geometry is supplied to the model or represented separately, and the model predicts fields on that geometry. The Manifold Function Encoder (MFE) provides an injective ambient representation of manifold–function pairs through manifold-supported measures and finite moments [15]. Its dual-norm error bound, however, does not by itself provide a local physical inverse or certify the topology of a reconstructed interface. These limitations leave two needs: a general approximation principle for continuous operators between geometric state sets and a stable physical decoder for jointly predicted interface–function states. In this paper, we first establish an approximation theorem for continuous operators between general state sets. Unlike earlier results based on Schauder bases [16], the theorem assumes injective representations of the input and output state sets in normed spaces, together with finite-dimensional encoding and reconstruction maps. We then introduce the Interface Autoencoder (IAE) for oriented interface–function states set as a concrete instance of this approximation framework. The IAE combines an injective fixed-box representation based on the signed-distance field [32] and the midpoint McShane–Whitney extension [30] with finite projection codes and a zero-set-based physical decoder. The IAE admits quantitative reconstruction bounds and geometric guarantees that support stable decoding. For tensor cosine codes, classical Jackson–Lebesgue estimates provide a uniform 

C0 reconstruction rate. We further establish a quantitative local gradient error bound for the unfiltered tensor cosine projection of a signed-distance function (Proposition 6.3(ii)). The estimate holds throughout a fixed tubular neighborhood, without requiring global smoothness of the distance function. Under suitable geometric regularity, these estimates yield Hausdorff and graph-Hausdorff error bounds and a reference-based topology certificate ensuring that the decoded interface is a unique C1 normal graph over the target. Several numerical experiments are conducted to verify the effectiveness of the IAE. In the cross-space setting, a variable-domain Poisson problem compares IAE encodings with moment encoders under a common operator architecture. In the within-space setting, IAE-based neural operators approximate Hele–Shaw flow containing mergers and jointly predict interface–surfactant states in two-phase Stokes flow.

# 8 Conclusion 

This work studies operators whose inputs or outputs contain interface–function states. We first established a general approximation principle that uses injective embeddings and convergent finite-dimensional codecs, without assuming linear state spaces or Schauder bases. We then instantiated this principle with the IAE. Signed-distance and midpoint McShane–Whitney extension fields provide an injective fixed-domain representation without interface parametrization; orthogonal projections provide deterministic codes, and zero-set extraction and restriction decode physical states. Under the stated regularity conditions, ambient approximation controls the reconstructed geometry and surface field and provides a reference-based topology certificate. The variable-domain Poisson problem examines cross-space prediction, while the Hele–Shaw and Stokes–surfactant problems examine within-space evolution of interfaces and their carried fields. These numerical results support the IAE as a common representation for geometric operator learning. Several directions remain open. On the theoretical side, the current results rely on pullback continuity and uniform output regularity, while the reference-based topology certificate excludes merger and breakup instants. Establishing PDE-specific conditions for these assumptions, developing stability guarantees across topology changes, and deriving finite-data generalization bounds are therefore important next steps. For evolutionary problems, the current time-conditioned models predict states at prescribed times without learning an explicit latent evolution law. Coupling IAE codes with continuous-time dynamics could enforce dissipation mechanisms consistent with thermodynamic principles [5, 49] and potentially enable complete error analyses [48]. The framework could also be extended to in-context learning [22, 44], using IAE codes to represent geometric states in data prompts. Extending the framework to higher-dimensional moving surfaces, multiple interacting interfaces, strongly coupled bulk–surface systems, and more complex topology changes would help assess its scalability and clarify the need for localized or adaptive representations. 23 Numerical implementation details
