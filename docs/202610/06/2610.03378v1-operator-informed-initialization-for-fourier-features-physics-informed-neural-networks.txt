A B S T R A C T
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) typically exhibit spectral bias, where some
frequencies of the target function converge more slowly than others. In this work, we analyze the
training dynamics of Fourier Feature PINNs in the Neural Tangent Kernel regime to address this
limitation. We derive an explicit evolution equation to estimate the residual error in the frequency
domain, demonstrating that the convergence rate of specific frequencies is primarily governed
by the product of the differential operator’s symbol and the spectral density of the initialization
weights. Leveraging this theoretical insight, we propose an informative initialization strategy that
tailors the initial weight distribution to the specific PDE being solved. With this method, we can
diminish the operator-induced spectral bias, balancing the convergence rates across the frequency
spectrum and achieving better prediction accuracy. Numerical experiments on linear and nonlinear
partial differential equations confirm that this initialization strategy improves learning dynamics
and approximation accuracy across frequencies compared to standard initialization methods, with
no additional training cost.
Introduction
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have emerged as a powerful framework for approximating solutions
of partial differential equations (PDEs) by embedding the governing physical laws directly into the training objective
(Raissi, Perdikaris and Karniadakis, 2019; Luo, Zhao, Wang, Li, Wen, Liang, Soekmadji and Liao, 2025). By combining
data-driven learning with the minimization of PDE residuals, PINNs provide a flexible alternative to classical numerical
solvers. Their versatility has enabled successful applications across a broad range of scientific domains, including
fluid mechanics (Raissi, Yazdani and Karniadakis, 2020; de Wolff, Carrillo, Martí and Sanchez-Pi, 2021; Bounnah,
Mihoubi and Larbi, 2025) and bioengineering (Sahli Costabal, Yang, Perdikaris, Hurtado and Kuhl, 2020; Kissas,
Yang, Hwuang, Witschey, Detre and Perdikaris, 2020). Despite these achievements, PINNs often suffer from slow
convergence and reduced predictive accuracy when the target solution exhibits sharp gradients, localized structures,
or highly oscillatory behavior (Wang, Wang and Perdikaris, 2021; Deshpande, Agarwal and Bhattacharya, 2022).
Increasing evidence suggests that these difficulties are closely related to the intrinsic frequency bias of neural networks.
Frequency Bias (FB), also known as spectral bias or the frequency principle, refers to the tendency of neural
networks trained with gradient-based methods to learn low-frequency components of a target function earlier and
faster than high-frequency ones (Xu, 2018; Luo, Ma, Xu and Zhang, 2019; Rahaman, Baratin, Arpit, Draxler, Lin,
Hamprecht, Bengio and Courville, 2019). This phenomenon has been observed across a wide range of architectures
(Geifman, Galun, Jacobs and Ronen, 2022; Bhattamishra, Patel, Kanade and Blunsom, 2022) and optimization methods
(Khodakarami, Oommen, Daryakenari, Beekenkamp and Karniadakis, 2026), including quasi-Newton approaches such

1. Background
Frequency Bias: Theoretical investigations of FB began with the analysis of shallow neural networks with smooth
activation functions such as tanh (Xu, 2018). Subsequent works studied the phenomenon through eigenvalue analyses
and assumptions on data distributions (Ronen, Jacobs, Kasten and Kritchman, 2019; Cao, Fang, Wu, Zhou and Gu,
2021), while others provided exact or asymptotic characterizations in the infinite-width regime (Luo, Ma, Xu and Zhang,
2022b; Luo, Ma, Wang, Xu and Zhang, 2022a; Zhang, Luo, Ma and Xu, 2021). Beyond discrete spectral analyses based
on kernel eigenvalues, continuous Fourier-domain descriptions of the learning dynamics have also been developed,
enabling a more refined understanding of how individual frequency modes evolve during training (Molina, Petrache,
Costabal and Courdurier, 2026; Luo et al., 2022b).
In the context of PINNs, frequency bias manifests in a more intricate manner due to the composite structure of the
loss function, which combines PDE residual terms with boundary, initial, and data constraints. It has been observed
that different components of the PINN loss may converge at substantially different rates when analyzed through their
limiting NTK dynamics (Wang, Yu and Perdikaris, 2022). Furthermore, frequency-dependent effects specific to PINNs
have been reported in Xu, Zhang and Cai (2025).
Fourier Feature Networks in PINNs: Several architectures have been proposed to mitigate the spectral bias of
standard neural networks, including Fourier Feature (FF) networks, SIREN, Gaussian networks, and WIRE (Tancik,
Srinivasan, Mildenhall, Fridovich-Keil, Raghavan, Singhal, Ramamoorthi, Barron and Ng, 2020; Sitzmann, Martel,
Bergman, Lindell and Wetzstein, 2020; Ramasinghe and Lucey, 2022; Saragadam, LeJeune, Tan, Balakrishnan,
Veeraraghavan and Baraniuk, 2023). While all these approaches improve the representation of high-frequency
components, FF networks are particularly attractive for the present work because they provide an explicit and controllable
parametrization of the network’s frequency content. In an FF network, the input coordinates are mapped through
sinusoidal functions with frequencies sampled from a prescribed distribution. Consequently, the spectral properties
of the representation can be modified directly through this distribution, without changing the subsequent network
architecture. Moreover, FF mappings admit a natural interpretation in the NTK regime, where the frequency distribution
controls the spectral properties and effective bandwidth of the kernel (Tancik et al., 2020; Molina et al., 2026). This
explicit connection between initialization, spectral representation, and training dynamics makes FF networks especially
suitable for incorporating prior information about the frequency geometry induced by a PDE.
Within the PINN framework, FF networks have been successfully applied to a variety of scientific and engineering
problems, including wind turbine power prediction (Aravanis, Papadopoulos and Georgikos, 2025) and forward and
inverse fluid mechanics problems (Sallam and Fürth, 2023). Several FF-based variants have also been proposed to
address multiscale and highly oscillatory PDEs. Examples include multiscale Fourier feature mappings for acoustic
wave equations (Chai, Cao, Li, Long and Sun, 2024), iterative basis-enhancement strategies (Wu, Aguiar, Johansson
and Barreau, 2025), finite-difference FF-PINNs for nonlinear fluid dynamics problems (Vasheghani Farahani, Tahsini,
Nematollahi and Ghiasi Afzal, 2026), and FF-PINNs for time-domain wave equations (Ding, Chen, Miyake and Li, 2025;
Wu, Yuan, Gao, Yang and Hou, 2026). More sophisticated approaches include spatially adaptive Fourier encodings
(Liu, Gu, Yu and Qin, 2025), natural frequency-based embeddings that align the representation spectrum with the
physical frequency content of the underlying system (Wang, Du, Li and Luo, 2026b), Fourier feature-based domain
decomposition methods (Li, Xia, Liu and Liao, 2023), and residual-based frequency modeling techniques (Hou, Bai,
Jing and lin Huang, 2026).
From a functional perspective, FF networks can also be viewed as a particular instance of physics-informed kernel
function neural networks (Fu, Xu and Liu, 2024), where a fixed basis expansion is combined with a trainable neural
architecture. This interpretation naturally connects FF-PINNs with kernel methods and motivates their analysis in the
NTK regime.
Neural Tangent Kernel Regime: When the width of the hidden layers tends to infinity, neural networks trained by
gradient descent admit a deterministic kernel description through the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) (Jacot, Gabriel
and Hongler, 2018; Lee, Xiao, Schoenholz, Bahri, Novak, Sohl-Dickstein and Pennington, 2019; Arora, Du, Hu, Li,
Salakhutdinov and Wang, 2019b; Arora, Du, Hu, Li and Wang, 2019a). In this regime, the network output evolves

The NTK framework has been successfully employed to study PINN training dynamics (Wang et al., 2022) and to
analyze how architectural modifications influence spectral convergence rates (Wang et al., 2021). The NTK framework
has become one of the principal tools for analyzing the learning dynamics of NNs ((Tancik et al., 2020)).
Related Work: Several strategies have been proposed to mitigate frequency bias in PINNs through improved
initialization procedures and frequency-aware training schemes. PDE-agnostic initialization methods have been
introduced to stabilize and accelerate training independently of the underlying operator (Liu, Zhang, Peng, Zhou
and Yao, 2022). Other approaches first pretrain a neural network without physics constraints and subsequently transfer
the learned weights to initialize the PINN, aiming to provide a more favorable spectral starting point for optimization
(Tarbiyati and Nemati Saray, 2024).
Beyond initialization, a variety of frequency-domain techniques have been developed to alleviate spectral imbalance.
These include methods that transform high-frequency components into lower-frequency representations before training
(Cai, Li and Liu, 2020; Cai, Zhu, Shen, Wang and Cao, 2024), Fourier feature-based domain decomposition approaches
that reduce high-frequency content locally (Li et al., 2023), and architectures specifically designed to address frequency
bias in PINNs (Xu et al., 2025). Other works propose adaptive or spatially varying Fourier feature encodings that
progressively expose higher frequencies during training (Liu et al., 2025).
Although these methods often demonstrate improved empirical performance, they are commonly tailored to
particular PDE classes, architectures, or training settings, and typically rely on problem-specific or heuristic frequency
manipulations. In contrast, our approach provides a unified framework in which the frequency-dependent dynamics are
derived directly from the differential operator. This allows the same initialization principle to be applied across a broad
range of linear and nonlinear PDEs by adapting only to their symbol, without redesigning the architecture or training
procedure for each problem. Moreover, the resulting PDE-informed distribution is supported by a frequency-domain
characterization of the residual dynamics, providing a more general and theoretically grounded mechanism for mitigating
PDE-induced frequency bias.
Main Contributions
• We derive a theoretical framework describing the residual frequency dynamics of PINNs with 2-layer Fourier
Feature networks in the NTK regime. The resulting dynamics explicitly reveal the interaction between the initial
parameter density distribution and the Fourier symbol of the PDE linear operator with constant coefficients.
Moreover, the convergence of the residual is strongly influenced by frequency regions where the PDE symbol
is small or vanishes. This characterization provides a principled explanation of how the differential operator
contributes to FB in PINNs.
• Motivated by the theoretical analysis, we propose a novel PDE-informed initialization strategy that adapts the
sampling distribution of parameters of the first layer according to the geometry induced by the PDE symbol.
• We validate the theoretical predictions through extensive numerical experiments on several PDEs. The proposed
initialization consistently mitigates frequency bias, accelerates optimization, improves residual convergence, and
reduces variability across random initialization seeds.
• We demonstrate that the proposed methodology remains effective beyond the theoretical assumptions used in the
analysis, including nonlinear PDEs, deep networks, and ADAM optimization. Furthermore, we show that the
proposed initialization can be integrated into existing PINN frameworks, such as JAX-PI, thereby improving
performance without incurring additional computational cost during training.

The PDE symbol 𝑝pde introduces an additional frequency-dependent modulation through the interior residual term.
Frequencies for which 𝑝pde(𝝃) is small receive only a weak contribution from the PDE residual, whereas frequencies
associated with larger values of 𝑝pde(𝝃) are strongly constrained by the interior equation. Importantly, 𝑝pde characterizes

corresponding frequency components depends entirely on the remaining boundary, initial-condition, and boundary-
integral terms. Thus, the interior differential operator identifies spectral directions that are weakly constrained by the
PDE residual, while the auxiliary conditions provide the additional information required to determine the physically
admissible solution (Evans, 2010).

Remark 3. A practical consideration is the choice of the bounded frequency domain used for rejection sampling.
Throughout this work, we use the Nyquist frequency associated with the computational grid, as it represents the largest
frequency that can be learned without aliasing. However, this bound is determined solely by the spatial discretization
and does not necessarily reflect the actual spectral content of the PDE solution. For example, the multiscale oscillatory
solution of the wave equation contains frequencies only up to 5.5, whereas the corresponding Nyquist frequency is
approximately 50. Consequently, the rejection sampling procedure allocates probability over a frequency range that is
substantially larger than necessary. Restricting the sampling domain to a frequency range better matched to the solution
could improve the efficiency of the proposed initialization by concentrating Fourier features in the relevant spectral
region. Nevertheless, the Nyquist frequency provides a simple, problem-independent choice that proved sufficiently
adequate for all the experiments considered in this work.

6.2. Training dynamics and solution accuracy
A small empirical PINN loss does not necessarily imply an accurate approximation of the underlying PDE solution.
The loss measures violations of the PDE and auxiliary constraints at the selected collocation points, whereas the relative
𝐿2 error measures the discrepancy with the reference solution over the computational domain. This mismatch can lead
to inaccurate or spurious solutions despite apparently successful optimization (Wang, Koohy, Lu and Perdikaris, 2026a).
The geometry of the region in frequency space identified in Section 5 also helps explain why favorable training
dynamics do not necessarily imply accurate predictions. In particular, modes near the zero-symbol set pde (see equation
29) are weakly constrained by the interior residual, while modes on pde receive no direct interior contribution. Their
amplitudes must therefore be selected by the boundary and initial-condition terms together with the empirical collocation
constraints.
The initialization scale 𝜎𝒘influences which portions of these weakly constrained spectral regions are effectively
represented by the network. For the transport equation, where pde is the characteristic line 𝜉𝜏+ 𝑐𝜉𝑥= 0, changing
𝜎𝒘modifies the spectral coverage around and along this direction. This may expose the network to high-frequency
modes that incur little interior PDE penalty. Such modes can enrich the approximation space but may also yield low-loss
solutions with poor predictive accuracy if they are not sufficiently constrained by the remaining terms.
This behavior is observed empirically in the multiscale transport problem: Gaussian initialization with 𝜎𝒘∈[8, 12]
frequently yields solutions with small training loss but large prediction error, whereas 𝜎𝒘∈[2, 6] produces substantially
more reliable approximations. A similar sensitivity is also observed for the PDE-informed initialization (Figure 4).
This observation also motivates the search ranges used for 𝜎𝒘in our hyperparameter optimization. The bounds
are selected empirically to avoid regimes that repeatedly exhibit low-training-loss but inaccurate solutions, rather than
being arbitrary numerical choices.

Figure 5 presents the gradient descent training dynamics under both initialization strategies. For the Laplace equation,
the two initializations exhibit comparable dynamics. Moderate but consistent improvements are observed for the heat

sets are isotropic, as in the Laplace equation, the PDE-informed distribution remains qualitatively similar to radial
Gaussian sampling. Introducing anisotropy, as in the heat equation, produces a more structured initialization and leads to
a clearer improvement. The largest differences arise when pde itself has a nontrivial geometry, such as the characteristic
lines or branches of the transport and wave equations or the circular zero set of the symbol of Helmholtz equation.
Overall, the observed gains are closely related to the mismatch between the geometry pde induced by 𝑝pde and the
geometry of isotropic Gaussian sampling.

We next investigate whether the previous results extend to nonlinear equations. In particular, we consider semilinear

information used to construct the PDE-informed initialization.

𝒘improves predictive

7. Discussion
In this work, we developed a theoretical framework describing the residual frequency dynamics of PINNs with
Fourier-feature neural networks in the NTK regime. Under the assumption of frozen Fourier frequencies, the dynamics
are governed by equation (27), which explicitly reveals the interaction between the initialization density (𝜌𝒘) and the
PDE symbol (𝑝pde). The analysis shows that the convergence rate of the residual is strongly influenced by regions of
frequency space where the PDE symbol becomes small or vanishes. In particular, the dimension and geometry of the
zero set pde = {𝝃∶𝑝pde(𝝃) = 0} determine the spectral directions along which the interior PDE residual provides
the weakest control. As a consequence, the spectral properties of both the differential operator and the initialization
distribution play a fundamental role in determining the optimization dynamics of PINNs.
Motivated by this observation, we proposed a PDE-informed initialization strategy that adapts the sampling
distribution of Fourier frequencies to the geometry induced by the PDE symbol. The proposed initialization increases
the concentration of frequencies near regions where 𝑝pde is small, thereby compensating for the uneven convergence
rates predicted by the theory. Unlike standard Gaussian initialization, which samples frequencies isotropically, the
proposed approach explicitly incorporates information about the underlying differential operator into the initialization
process.
The theoretical predictions were validated through extensive numerical experiments involving PDEs with markedly
different geometries of pde. Across a broad range of examples, the proposed initialization consistently mitigated
frequency bias, accelerated optimization, improved residual convergence, and reduced variability across random seeds.
The magnitude of the improvement was closely associated with the dimension and shape of pde, as well as with the
geometry of the surrounding regions where 𝑝pde remains small. For the Laplace equation, pde reduces to the origin
and the two initialization strategies exhibit comparable dynamics. The heat equation also has an isolated zero, but
its low-symbol region is anisotropic, leading to moderate improvements. In contrast, substantially larger gains are

observed for the transport, wave, and Helmholtz equations, where pde contains extended structures in frequency
space. These results suggest that the advantage of the PDE-informed initialization becomes more pronounced when the
weakly constrained spectral region has a nontrivial geometry that is not efficiently captured by an isotropic Gaussian
initialization.
Furthermore, the empirical results indicate that the proposed methodology extends well beyond the theoretical
assumptions used in the analysis. In particular, the initialization remains effective for nonlinear PDEs, multilayer neural
network architectures, and ADAM optimization. We also demonstrated that the proposed initialization can improve the
performance of established PINN frameworks, such as JAX-PI, without incurring additional computational cost, since
the method only modifies the sampling distribution used during initialization.
Compared with previous approaches to mitigating frequency bias in PINNs, the proposed methodology differs in
both its motivation and its design. Existing initialization strategies are generally PDE-agnostic, aiming to improve
optimization through generic weight initialization or transfer learning without explicitly exploiting the structure of
the governing equation (Liu et al., 2022; Tarbiyati and Nemati Saray, 2024). Other frequency-aware methods instead
modify the network architecture, progressively introduce higher frequencies during training, or transform the learning
problem to facilitate the optimization of oscillatory solutions (Cai et al., 2020, 2024; Li et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025;
Liu et al., 2025). In contrast, our approach directly incorporates information from the PDE via its Fourier symbol,
yielding an initialization distribution tailored to the spectral structure of the underlying differential operator. Moreover,
unlike methods that alter the training procedure or network architecture, the proposed strategy is implemented solely at
initialization, making it straightforward to integrate into existing PINN frameworks.
Despite these promising results, several limitations remain. The most important one is that the proposed PDE-
informed initialization relies on the availability of a meaningful PDE symbol (𝑝pde). Consequently, the current
methodology is naturally applicable to linear and semilinear PDEs with constant coefficients, where the principal linear
operator provides a well-defined frequency characterization of the problem. For fully nonlinear PDEs, however, there is
generally no unique linear symbol that captures the dominant dynamics, making the construction of a PDE-informed
frequency distribution considerably more challenging. Extending the present framework to such settings is an important
direction for future research and may require developing nonlinear or solution-dependent frequency descriptors.
More broadly, the framework developed in this paper suggests a general principle for neural network initialization:
optimization can be improved by aligning the initialization distribution with the spectral structure of the operator
governing the learning problem. From this perspective, the methodology introduced here may extend beyond PINNs to
inverse problems, integral and nonlocal equations, operator-learning architectures, and other machine-learning settings
in which the optimization dynamics are shaped by the spectral properties of an underlying operator. We hope these
results contribute to a deeper understanding of frequency bias in scientific machine learning and provide a foundation
for developing operator-aware training strategies for future neural network models.

B. Effect of PDE-symbol mismatch
To further assess the role of the PDE information in the proposed initialization, we consider a transport example,
for which the true transport velocity is 𝑐= 1. We compare the standard Gaussian initialization with four PDE-informed
initializations. The first is constructed using the correct transport velocity, 𝑐= 1, while the remaining three use
deliberately incorrect values, 𝑐= 2, 𝑐= 5, and 𝑐= 10.
Importantly, the transport equation used to define the training loss is identical in all experiments and always uses
the true value 𝑐= 1. Only the polynomial employed to construct the PDE-informed initialization is changed. Using an
incorrect value of 𝑐therefore rotates this preferred region in frequency space away from the characteristic set of the
actual PDE.
Figure 12 shows the training loss and the relative 𝐿2 prediction error for the different initializations. The PDE-
informed initialization, with the correct value 𝑐= 1, achieves substantially faster training dynamics and lower prediction
error than all alternatives. When 𝑐= 2 is used to construct the initialization, the resulting performance is already much
closer to that of the standard Gaussian initialization. As the assumed velocity deviates further from the true value, with
𝑐= 5 and 𝑐= 10, both the optimization dynamics and prediction accuracy progressively deteriorate.
These results provide additional evidence that the improvement produced by the proposed initialization is specifically
associated with the PDE-dependent frequency geometry encoded by 𝑝pde, rather than merely with using a non-Gaussian
frequency distribution. In particular, among the tested initializations, the best performance is obtained when the
polynomial used for sampling matches the true differential operator, while increasing the mismatch between the assumed
and true characteristic sets leads to progressively worse dynamics.

C.2. Ablation study using PDE-informed initialization
To further evaluate the robustness of the proposed initialization, we performed an ablation study using two benchmark
problems from the JAX-PI repository: the Advection and Allen–Cahn equations. Tables 4 and 5 compare the PDE-
informed initialization under different combinations of the optimization strategies available in JAX-PI. The results
indicate that these additional techniques do not necessarily improve the performance of the proposed initialization,
suggesting that the PDE-informed sampling already provides a favorable starting point for optimization. Among the
tested components, gradient normalization appears to be the most important, consistently yielding faster convergence
while maintaining competitive prediction errors. In contrast, the time-period strategy is not compatible with the proposed
initialization for the Advection equation, leading to a substantial degradation in predictive accuracy when enabled.
Overall, these experiments suggest that the PDE-informed initialization can be effectively combined with existing
PINN training strategies, although not all combinations are complementary and some heuristics may interfere with the
optimization dynamics induced by the proposed frequency-aware initialization.
