Abstract 

Accurate numerical simulation of nonlinear partial differential equation initial value problems typically requires high-dimensional full-order discretizations at substantial computational cost. While purely data-driven surrogates can speed up prediction, these "black box" approximations do not necessarily satisfy the governing equations during inference. In this paper we present a hybrid framework, which combines neural operators with projection-based reduced-order modeling for nonlinear PDE simulation. The key idea is to train a neural operator to map the initial condition to a time-dependent reduced subspace, and then evolve low-dimensional reduced coordinates by solving the governing dynamical equations projected on this learned moving trial subspace. In this way, the network predicts an adaptive reduced rep-resentation rather than the full trajectory directly, while the online solver preserves a physics-based reduced evolution. We test this method on two representative nonlinear PDEs: the viscous Burgers’ equation and the Fisher–KPP reaction–diffusion equation. In both cases, the proposed approach achieves successful reduced-order simulation with substantial wall-clock speedup relative to the corresponding full-order solver while maintaining relative errors on the order of 10 −2 to 10 −1 across the tested resolutions. These results demonstrate that a low-dimensional learned time-dependent reduced subspace can be used to predict solutions to nonlinear PDEs while retaining the advantages of projection-based reduced

1. Introduction 

Direct numerical simulation of nonlinear time-dependent partial differen-tial equations (PDEs) is a central computational challenge in fluid dynam-ics, reaction-diffusion systems, transport phenomena, and scientific comput-ing more broadly. When a discretized PDE state has dimension N ≫ 1,repeated simulations for many initial conditions or parameters can be pro-hibitively expensive. This challenge is especially acute in settings such as design loops, inverse problems, uncertainty quantification, optimal control, and many-query forecasting, where one must repeatedly integrate the same governing equations over a family of inputs. Projection-based reduced-order modeling (ROM) seeks to reduce this cost by approximating the full-order solution in a low-dimensional subspace, typ-ically constructed offline from solution snapshots. Classical reduced order methods based on proper orthogonal decompositions (POD) and Galerkin or Petrov–Galerkin projections have demonstrated strong effectiveness in many settings and remain a foundational paradigm in model reduction [1–4]. How-ever, nonlinear transient dynamics often evolve on solution manifolds that are poorly approximated by a single fixed linear subspace. As a result, a static linear subspace may require many modes to accurately resolve evolving co-herent structures or nonstationary spatial features, thereby weakening the compression benefit. This limitation is especially prominent for transport-dominated or strongly nonstationary dynamics, where linear reduced sub-spaces can exhibit slow approximation-error decay [5, 6]. At the same time, machine learning methods provide powerful alterna-tives for learning solution operators directly from data. In particular, neural operators such as DeepONet and the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) have shown that maps between function spaces can be approximated efficiently and with strong empirical performance on PDE problems [7–9]. Yet di-rect surrogate prediction also has limitations: the resulting model may not explicitly preserve the structure of the governing equations, and long-time or out-of-distribution reliability may be difficult to guarantee. This limita-2tion motivates hybrid approaches that combine learned representations with physics-based reduced simulation. In this work, we develop such a hybrid strategy for nonlinear PDE initial value problems. Rather than directly learning the full solution trajectory, we train a neural operator to predict a time-dependent reduced subspace from the initial condition on which the reduced governing dynamics are solved. Thus, the machine learning component predicts where the solution should live, while the reduced solver determines how it evolves according to the PDE. The use of time-dependent subspaces is motivated by the observation that many nonlinear trajectories are more accurately approximated by a moving low-dimensional subspace than by a fixed linear subspace. Classical adaptive and time-dependent model reduction ideas have long recognized this point [4, 10, 11], and recent work has continued to explore time-dependent subspaces in specialized contexts [12, 13]. Our contribution is to place this idea in a neural-operator-guided setting for nonlinear PDE initial value problems: the time-varying reduced trial subspace is inferred from the initial condition before the online solve begins. We demonstrate the method on two canonical nonlinear PDEs: the vis-cous Burgers’ equation and the Fisher–KPP reaction–diffusion equation. In both cases, the proposed framework achieves substantial wall-clock speedup while maintaining final-time mean absolute relative errors on the order of 

10 −2 to 10 −1 across the tested resolutions. These experiments suggest that learning a time-dependent reduced subspace can provide a useful middle ground between reduced-order simulation and direct black-box trajectory re-gression. The main contributions of this paper are: • We propose a neural-operator-guided PDE reduced-order modeling frame-work that can be generalized across different initial condition fields and different spatial resolutions. • We introduce a formulation in which a neural operator predicts a low-dimensional time-dependent reduced subspace conditioned on the initial condition. • We demonstrate the method on two nonlinear benchmark problems, vis-cous Burgers’ equation and Fisher–KPP equation, and report substantial 3wall-clock speedup and final-time relative errors on the order of 10 −2 to 

10 −1.• We position the method relative to classical ROM, direct neural opera-tor surrogates, and recent hybrid ML–ROM approaches, and we identify practical directions for improving robustness and efficiency. 

2. Related Work 

2.1. Projection-based reduced-order modeling 

Projection-based reduced-order modeling approximates a high-dimensional state u(t) ∈ RN by a lower-dimensional representation in a reduced trial subspace. A standard starting point is POD, often implemented through the method of snapshots, which constructs an optimal linear subspace in the L2

sense from training data [1]. Reduced subspace methods extend this approach to parametrized PDEs with carefully designed offline/online decompositions and have been developed into a mature framework for both stationary and time-dependent problems [2–4]. Our work builds on this projection-based ROM method, but differs in one central respect: the reduced trial subspace is not fixed in advance or adapted online by intrusive updates. Instead, it is predicted from the initial condition by a trained neural operator and then used in a projected reduced simulation. 

2.2. Time-dependent and adaptive reduced spaces 

A static linear reduced subspace is often insufficient for transport-dominated or nonstationary dynamics. This issue has motivated a variety of localized adaptive and time-dependent subspace strategies. Online adaptive subspace updates for nonlinear systems have also been proposed, for example by cou-pling low-rank updates with hyper-reduction ideas [10]. Time-dependent subspace basis methods also appear in dynamically orthogonal and dynami-cally bi-orthogonal formulations for stochastic PDEs [11–13]. Recent work has further explored ROM with explicitly time-dependent subspaces in more specialized contexts, including stochastic PDEs and evolv-ing boundary-condition settings [12, 13]. These works reinforce the core premise that moving low-dimensional spaces can significantly improve ap-proximation efficiency when the relevant solution structures translate, de-form, or otherwise evolve in ways that are not well-aligned with a fixed 4subspace. Our method shares this perspective, but differs in that the time-dependent subspace is predicted from the initial condition by a neural op-erator rather than being evolved by a separate intrusive subspace-dynamics system. 

2.3. Neural operators and operator learning 

Neural operators can be used to learn maps between function spaces rather than maps between vector spaces with fixed finite dimensions. In par-ticular, Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) provide a highly effective spectral-kernel parameterization that has become a widely used architecture for PDE learning [8, 9]. These models can approximate families of PDE solution op-erators and, in many cases, offer a significant improvement in speed over conventional solvers. Most neural operator work, however, focuses on the direct prediction of solution fields or the one-step / multi-step evolution operators. In that set-ting, the learned models replace the numerical solvers. Instead, our work uses a neural operator to predict a time-dependent set of vectors, {ϕ1(t), ϕ 2(t), . . . , ϕ r(t)}

which generically span an r-dimensional trial subspace Λ( t). Thus, the net-work approximates a map from an initial condition to Λ( t)

u0(·) 7 → Λ( t) = span {ϕ1(t), ϕ 2(t), . . . , ϕ r(t)}

rather than directly approximating 

u0(·) 7 → u(t, ·).

This distinction is important: the online dynamics in our method remain governed by a projected version of the PDE. 

2.4. Hybrid machine learning and ROM approaches 

There is substantial recent literature on combining data-driven learning with reduced-order modeling. One approach focuses on learning reduced dynamics directly from snapshot data; for example, operator inference uses structured regression in reduced coordinates to identify low-dimensional dy-namical systems consistent with the underlying reduced model form [14, 15]. An alternative approach is based on learning latent dynamics using autoen-coders and nonlinear trial manifolds [16, 17]. Related hybrid ROM strategies have also combined projection-based models with neural corrections, physics-informed reduced dynamics, or POD–deep-learning prediction architectures [6, 18, 19]. 5Our approach is closest in spirit to hybrid methods that preserve a projection-based online solve while using machine learning to improve the reduced repre-sentation. However, our method differs from those in much of this literature in two significant ways. First, the learned object is a time-dependent linear trial subspace , instead of a latent coordinate update map or a nonlinear de-coder manifold. Second, this subspace is predicted directly from the initial condition at the field level by a neural operator, which naturally accommo-dates functional inputs and outputs. Moreover, using the projection-based online solver acting on the trial subspace, which is data-adaptive and input-conditioned, ensures that this design preserves more physical structure than direct black-box trajectory regression while achieving better compressibility than a fixed linear subspace on dynamical problems. 

3. Problem Formulation 

3.1. Nonlinear PDE initial value problems 

We consider nonlinear PDE initial value problems of the form 

∂tu(t, x) = F(u(t, x)) , x ∈ Ω, t ∈ [0 , T ], (1) with initial condition 

u(0 , x) = u0(x). (2) Here, u(t, x) denotes the state variable, Ω ⊂ Rd is the spatial domain, and 

F is a nonlinear differential operator. After spatial discretization, eq. (1) is approximated by a system of ODEs 

ddt u(t) = F (u(t)) , u(0) = u0, (3) where u(t) ∈ RNdof , and Ndof denotes the number of discrete degrees of freedom. 

3.2. Reduced approximation in a moving trial space 

We seek an approximation of the full-order state u(t) in a low-dimensional time-dependent trial space: 

u(t) ≈ Φ(t)a(t) + u0, (4) 6where 

Φ(t) = [ ϕ1(t), ϕ 2(t), . . . , ϕ r(t)] ∈ M at Ndof ×r(R), a(t) ∈ Rr, r ≪ Ndof .

The columns of the matrix Φ(t) are a time-dependent basis of an r-dimensional subspace Λ( t) ⊂ RNdof , and a(t) are the reduced coordinates that represent the evolution away from the initial condition. The key question is how to construct Φ(t) for a previously unseen initial condition u0 in a way that is both inexpensive and sufficiently accurate for reduced simulation. In standard ROM, one often selects a fixed matrix Φ from global training snapshots and projects eq. (3) onto the column space of Φ. Instead, we use a neural operator to predict a matrix Φ(t) that depends on both the initial condition u0 and time t.

4. Method 

4.1. Overview 

The proposed framework consists of the following stages: 1. Generate full-order solution trajectories for a training set of initial con-ditions. 2. Construct a time-dependent POD basis for a reduced subspace Λ( t) from these trajectories. 3. Train a neural operator to map an initial condition u0 to the matrix Φ(t)

whose columns are the time-dependent POD basis of Λ( t).4. For a new initial condition, evaluate the trained neural operator to predict 

Φ(t).5. Solve a reduced dynamical system for the coordinates a(t) in the predicted moving subspace. 6. Reconstruct the approximate full state via u(t) ≈ Φ(t)a(t) + u0.Conceptually, the framework separates representation learning from dy-namical evolution . It vanishes for a static subspace basis and is essential in the moving-basis setting. Intuitively, it accounts for the fact that part of the apparent change in the state arises from the motion of the trial space itself rather than from changes in the reduced coordinates. This term is standard in changing-coordinate formulations and must be retained for consistency. Similar correction terms arise naturally in time-dependent subspace basis and dynamically orthogonal formulations [11, 12]. 

4.5. Physics consistency of the projected evolution 

An important conceptual feature of the method is that the neural network does not directly replace the governing dynamics. Instead, it predicts a low-dimensional time-dependent trial space in which the governing equations are approximately enforced by projection. This is more physics-constrained than direct trajectory regression because the online state still results from integrating a reduced dynamical system derived from the PDE. At the same time, the method is more adaptive than standard ROM because the reduced space is conditioned on the particular initial condition field of interest. The remaining online cost consists of three parts: (1) inferencing the neural operator to obtain the subspace basis prediction; (2) building the reduced order model based on the predicted subspace basis; (3) integrating the reduced system eq. (12). 

5. Experimental Setup 

5.1. Test problems 5.1.1. Viscous Burgers’ equation 

As a first nonlinear benchmark, we consider the two-dimensional viscous Burgers’ equation [21] for a vector velocity field u(t, x) = ( u1(t, x), u 2(t, x)) ⊤

10 on the periodic domain Ω = [0 , 1] 2:

∂tu + ( u · ∇ )u = ν∆u, x = ( x, y ) ∈ Ω, (13) with periodic boundary conditions and initial condition 

u(0 , x) = u0(x). (14) Here ν > 0 denotes the viscosity. We use this problem as a representative nonlinear transport–diffusion system whose solution exhibits evolving spa-tial structures and nonlinear self-advection, making it a natural test case for time-dependent subspace bases. In the experiments, the initial velocity fields are generated as smooth random fields on the periodic domain using a band-limited Matérn Gaussian random field construction in Fourier space. This process is explained with more detail in the Appendix. Burgers’ equa-tion provides a useful benchmark because it contains both nonlinear advec-tion and diffusive smoothing, and its solutions often exhibit moving coherent structures that are difficult to compress accurately with a single static linear subspace. 

5.1.2. Fisher–KPP Equation 

As a second nonlinear benchmark, we consider the two-dimensional Fisher– KPP reaction–diffusion equation [22] on the periodic domain Ω = [0 , 1] 2:

∂tu = D∆u + ρ u (1 − u), x ∈ Ω, (15) with periodic boundary conditions and initial condition 

u(0 , x) = u0(x). (16) Here D > 0 is the diffusion constant and ρ > 0 is the reaction strength. In the data-generation process, the full-order simulations are run with D = 0 .01 ,

ρ = 0 .8, time step ∆t = 10 −3, and final time T = 1 . The full-order solver uses an IMEX Euler scheme, with diffusion treated implicitly and the logistic reaction term treated explicitly [23, 24]. The initial condition fields are generated from smooth low-frequency ran-dom fields on the periodic domain. More specifically, a symmetry-enforced low-frequency Fourier random field is used, with domain length L = 1 , target standard deviation σ = 0 .2, and Fourier cutoff kmax = 8 . Each sampled field is then rescaled pointwise to lie in a prescribed interval u0(x) ∈ [0 .02 , 0.2] ,which ensures nonnegative and spatially smooth initial data while maintain-ing diversity across trajectories. 11 5.2. 5.3. Neural operator training details 

The neural operator is trained to predict time-dependent reduced sub-space matrices at a finite number of temporal breakpoints. For the Burgers’ equation case, we use a multi-resolution Fourier Neural Operator with 5 lay-ers, 150 hidden channels, and Fourier truncation nmodes = (32 , 32) . The input has 4 channels and the output has 40 channels. The model contains approx-imately 1.23 × 10 8 trainable parameters. For the Fisher–KPP equation case, we likewise use a 5-layer Fourier Neural Operator with 150 hidden channels, but with a smaller spectral truncation nmodes = (16 , 16) . The input has 3

channels and the output has 18 channels. This model contains approximately 

3.27 × 10 7 trainable parameters. In both cases, the neural operators are trained with AdamW using learn-ing rate 10 −3, weight decay 10 −4, and a cosine annealing learning-rate sched-ule over 200 epochs [25, 26]. The training is carried out in a multi-resolution fashion by sampling batches from 64 2, 128 2, and 256 2 datasets. The loss used in training is a weighted mixed relative H1 and relative L2 loss on the predicted reduced POD matrices channels. 

5.4. Evaluation protocol 

The principal reference is the corresponding full-order model (FOM). For each test case, the trained neural operator first predicts the time-dependent reduced subspace, after which the ROM is integrated forward in time in the predicted moving trial subspace. We then compare the reconstructed ROM trajectory against the full-order trajectory.

6.3. Summary of numerical results 

Taken together, the Burgers’ equation and Fisher–KPP equation experi-ments support the central idea of this paper: instead of learning the full order solution trajectory directly, it is often sufficient to learn a low-dimensional time-dependent trial subspace and then evolve the reduced coordinates by solving a projected dynamical system in that subspace. This hybrid strat-egy retains the physics-based structure of a ROM solve while using a neural operator to adapt the reduced subspace to the specific initial condition. In both examples, this leads to accurate trajectories together with meaningful online speedup relative to the corresponding full-order simulation. 17 7. Discussion 

7.1. When the method is likely to work well 

The proposed framework is likely to be most effective when the solution trajectories have only a few dominant spatial modes, but which are strongly initial condition-dependent. This includes settings where the dominant struc-tures evolve or translate over time, making a fixed global basis inefficient, yet where the governing dynamics still admit a compact representation in a mov-ing linear space. Burgers’ equation and Fisher–KPP equation both fit this picture to a meaningful extent. The method is especially appealing in many-query regimes where the same PDE must be solved repeatedly for varying initial data. In such settings, the offline training cost can be amortized, and the online speedup becomes operationally relevant. 

7.2. Advantages 

This framework offers several advantages: • It uses a PDE-based online ROM solver to model the time evolution rather than a black-box predictor. • It allows the reduced trial space to depend on the initial condition, im-proving adaptivity relative to a fixed basis ROM. • It leverages neural operators for field-to-field prediction, enabling gener-alization across different spatial resolutions. • It is modular: improvements in basis extraction, neural operator architec-tures, temporal interpolation, or hyper-reduction can all be incorporated without changing the overall method. 

7.3. Limitations 

Several limitations remain. First, although the reduced subspace is low-dimensional, evaluating nonpolynomial or nonlinear terms in eq. (12) may still require full-order operations unless hyper-reduction is incorporated, for example through EIM-, DEIM-, GNAT-, or empirical-cubature-style approx-imations [27–29]. Thus the present method improves efficiency but does not yet realize the full online complexity reductions available in advanced non-linear ROM pipelines. 18 Second, basis prediction is subject to ambiguities in sign, ordering, and rotation, especially when multiple modes have similar importance. A simple matrix-norm training loss may not optimally respect the underlying subspace geometry. This suggests using losses or optimization formulations defined on Stiefel or Grassmann manifolds [30, 31]. Third, the present numerical study, while encouraging, is still limited in scope. Broader validation on more challenging PDE families and stronger baselines would be needed to fully establish the strengths and limitations of this approach. 

7.4. Future work 

Several directions appear especially promising: • integrating hyper-reduction, such as EIM-, DEIM-, GNAT-, or empirical-cubature-style ideas, into the moving-basis ROM; • designing subspace-specific training losses that are invariant to basis sign and within-subspace rotations; • learning temporally coherent basis parameterizations rather than predict-ing independent basis snapshots at breakpoints; • incorporating structure-preserving constraints, especially for conservative or Hamiltonian systems; • extending the framework to parameterized PDE families beyond initial-condition variation. 

8. Conclusion 

We have presented a hybrid framework for nonlinear PDE initial value problems in which a neural operator predicts a low-dimensional time-dependent reduced subspace from the initial condition, and the reduced coordinates are then evolved by solving a projected reduced dynamical system in this moving trial space. The method combines two complementary ingredients: expres-sive function-to-function learning for basis prediction, and a physics-based reduced solver for dynamical evolution. Numerical experiments on the viscous Burgers’ equation and the Fisher– KPP equation show that the proposed method achieves substantial wall-clock speedups that increase with spatial resolution, reaching up to 76 .42 × for 19 Burgers’ equation and 37 .44 × for Fisher–KPP equation, while maintaining final-time mean absolute relative errors on the order of 10 −2 to 10 −1 across tested cases. These results support the view that learning an adaptive re-duced trial space, rather than the full trajectory directly, is a viable strategy for data-assisted simulation of nonlinear PDEs. More broadly, the method highlights a promising interface between neural operators and reduced-order modeling. Instead of treating machine learning and numerical model reduction as competing approaches, it uses learning to improve the reduced representation while preserving a projection-based dy-namical core. We expect that richer basis representations, stronger subspace-aware learning objectives, and additional hyper-reduction will further im-prove the method’s efficiency and robustness.
