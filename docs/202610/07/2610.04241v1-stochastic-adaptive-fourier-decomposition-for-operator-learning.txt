## Abstract 

Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) offer an efficient paradigm for solving partial dif-ferential equations (PDEs). However, FNOs rely on a fixed Fourier basis and hard frequency truncation, which inherently limit their ability to model non-periodic, localized, and fine-scale solution structures. We propose the Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition Neural Operator (SAFDNO), a spectral neural operator that replaces predefined Fourier modes with an adaptive Takenaka-Malmquist (TM) orthonormal system derived from the theory of Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition (SAFD). Instead of performing an expensive greedy pole search in classical SAFD, SAFDNO amortizes stochastic pole selection through a neural pole predictor and constructs the adaptive TM system directly from latent features. The resulting operator performs learned filtering on coefficients of analytic branches in the Hardy space with respect to the input-adaptive TM system, preserving the global receptive field of spectral operators while providing a more flexible repre-sentation than using fixed spectral bases. Across nine PDE benchmark problems, SAFDNO achieves the best performance on all six regular-grid problems among strong neural operator baselines, with especially notable gains on Darcy, Burgers, and Navier-Stokes, where fixed Fourier modes are often less effective at modeling localized oscillations, sharp transitions, and multiscale structures. SAFDNO also exhibits stronger zero-shot super-resolution performance and shows less perfor-mance degradation when deployed on finer discretizations. These results suggest that the input-adaptive TM system provides a promising alternative to fixed spectral representations for neural operator learning. 

## 1 Introduction 

Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) are fundamental mathematical tools for describing and modeling a wide range of physical and engineering systems, capturing how quantities evolve over time and space. Although traditional numerical methods are accurate, they can be computationally expensive when solving PDEs on complex geometries. Operator learning, which aims to learn the mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces, offers a more computationally efficient approach for solving various families of PDEs [1]. arXiv:2610.04241v1 [cs.LG] 3 Oct 2026 The Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) [2] is an effective and efficient operator learning framework that represents a global convolution operator, equivalently a translation-invariant integral kernel, in the Fourier domain through learned Fourier multipliers. In practice, it applies the fast Fourier transform (FFT) with frequency truncation, performs a convolution operator on the retained low-frequency Fourier coefficients, and then uses the inverse FFT (iFFT) to transform the representation back to the physics domain. This architecture design equips FNO with efficient global receptive fields and discretization-invariant learning, allowing the learned operator to generalize across different discretizations. In particular, FNO can be trained on coarse grids and directly generalize to finer grids, thereby enabling zero-shot super-resolution.

Nevertheless, the use of frequency truncation and a fixed Fourier transform in FNO may hinder the expressive power and performance on certain PDEs. Specifically, frequency truncation may violate the equivalence of continuous and discrete representations in neural operators, thereby introducing aliasing effects [3]. In addition, truncating higher-frequency modes may induce spectral bias and limit the ability to capture fine-scale solution structures, particularly in PDEs with strong localized high-frequency oscillations [4]. Moreover, the Fourier transform used in FNO inherently relies on the assumptions of Fourier analysis. As a result, dependence on a fixed Fourier basis may be inefficient for PDEs with non-periodic patterns, localized fine-scale structures, sharp discontinuities, and non-stationary dynamics [5, 6]. Recent studies have sought to address these limitations from several directions. Some extend FNO within the Fourier framework by improving Fourier-domain kernel operator layers to alleviate the spectral bias induced by fixed mode truncation [4], enriching Fourier layers with local convolution pathways to improve local and high-frequency representation [7], or extending FNO to irregular geometries through learned deformations [8]. Others replace the Fourier transform with alternative predefined transforms or bases, such as wavelet, Laplace, or Hilbert representations, to better capture multiscale structures, transient signals, or phase information [5, 6, 9–11]. However, most existing approaches still rely on fixed spectral representations, rather than learning an input-adaptive basis from data, which may still inherently constrain the ability to learn non-periodic, localized high-frequency, non-stationary, and multiscale components. To overcome the aforementioned limitations, we introduce the Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decom-position Neural Operator (SAFDNO), motivated by the framework of Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition (SAFD) [12–16]. Unlike the Fourier transform, which expands signals over a fixed set of global sinusoidal bases, SAFD adaptively selects rational orthogonal functions associated with a Takenaka-Malmquist (TM) system according to input signals [12, 13]. Due to this adaptive mechanism, the SAFD method often exhibits faster energy convergence and can capture oscillatory 2structures and higher-frequency components more flexibly and efficiently than the fixed Fourier transform. Moreover, the SAFD method generally requires fewer poles than Fourier modes to achieve a comparable approximation error. These properties suggest that SAFD can provide a more flexible and parsimonious representation for PDE solution operators. As the representative example shown in Figure 1, Navier–Stokes turbulence manifests localized and multiscale dynamics, energy resides at large scales, while enstrophy concentrates on thin filamentary sets; our SAFDNO shows a stronger performance on learning non-stationary, high-frequency, and multiscale patterns. Building on this perspective, our SAFDNO learns an input-dependent TM system representation directly from data, instead of relying on a predefined transform with explicit frequency truncation. In contrast to fixed spectral bases, the learned TM system adapts to input signals and allocates represen-tation capacity adaptively according to pole placements, yielding a more flexible representation of the underlying structure for neural operator learning. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose the Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition Neural Operator (SAFDNO), which incorporates the adaptive Takenaka-Malmquist (TM) orthonormal system and Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition (SAFD) into spectral neural operator learning. • We provide a theoretical analysis of the representation capacity of SAFD, proving its advantages over fixed Fourier bases on PDEs with localized and multiscale structures. The effectiveness of SAFDNO is empirically validated on multiple PDE benchmarks against strong baselines. 

## 2 Related Work 

Operator learning. Operator learning is a data-driven framework that focuses on learning function-to-function mappings. DeepONet [17] is a representative architecture that parameterizes operators through branch and trunk networks. The FNO [2] parameterizes the global convolution by using a translation-invariant integral kernel in the Fourier domain and enables discretization-invariance. Attention-based operators, such as OFormer [18] and GNOT [19], improve flexibility on irregular grids and arbitrary query locations. Our work is closely related to spectral neural operators. 

Spectral neural operators. Building on FNO, a substantial body of work has advanced Fourier-based operator learning, yet still relies on the fixed Fourier transform. Geo-FNO [8] extends FNO to arbitrary geometries by learning a deformation from the physical domain to a latent regular grid. F-FNO [20] improves scalability through factorized spectral layers. AM-FNO [4] alleviates the spectral bias caused by frequency truncation by using an amortized neural network to generate learnable weights for kernel parameterization, thereby allowing the model to accommodate arbitrarily many modes with a fixed number of parameters. DSFNO [21] further enhances expressiveness through input-dependent dynamic kernels and interactions among Fourier modes. However, these methods are intrinsically constrained by the inherent limitations of Fourier analysis and are therefore less suitable for capturing multiscale, spatially localized features or non-periodic structures. This limitation motivates a reconsideration of the underlying transform or basis. Wavelet-based methods, such as WNO [11], exploit frequency localization to better capture spatially localized and fine-scale patterns. Multiwavelet-based operator learning projects the operator kernel onto multiwavelet polynomial bases and learns complex dependencies across multiple scales [10]. The Laplace neural operator [9] represents the kernel integral operator in the Laplace domain using a pole-residue formulation between the input and solution spaces, thereby improving the modeling of non-periodic signals. Nevertheless, most existing works predominately rely on predefined transforms or fixed basis systems that are independent of the input, whereas input-adaptive basis construction remains relatively underexplored in operator learning. 

Adaptive Fourier Decomposition. Adaptive Fourier Decomposition (AFD) and its stochastic variant (SAFD) were originally introduced by Qian et al. [12, 13, 16] as signal analysis methods that employ adaptive rational orthonormal systems and have been shown to be effective in analyzing oscillatory and non-stationary signals. In machine learning, AFD has been used for classification [22] and feature extraction [23], and SAFD has demonstrated strong compression performance in image analysis [24–26] and video applications [27, 28]. In operator learning, the Adaptive Mamba Operator (AMO) [29] incorporates AFD by reparameterizing transfer functions as TM basis functions. ## 4 Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition for Operator Learning 

Building on the SAFD framework, this section introduces the proposed Stochastic Adaptive Fourier Decomposition Neural Operator (SAFDNO). SAFDNO can be viewed as an adaptive neural operator that replaces the fixed Fourier basis used in FNO [2] with a data-dependent TM system. Specifically, instead of using a fixed Fourier basis with hard frequency truncation, the model first predicts poles adaptively in the unit disk, and then constructs the corresponding TM system according to adaptive pole placements. The key distinction from FNO is that the spectral components in SAFDNO are not fixed a priori but are adaptively inferred from the data, and the pole placements determine the localization strength of the resulting TM representations, enabling a more flexible and data-aligned representation. 

4.1 Neural Pole Predictor 

As discussed earlier, the classic stochastic AFD selects poles sequentially with a greedy procedure [13, 14], which chooses the pole that captures the largest amount of expected energy at each step. This recursive pole selection process is computationally expensive for operator learning. To address this issue, SAFDNO replaces this greedy search with a neural pole predictor that amortizes pole selection across samples. ## 5 Experiments 

This section presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation of the proposed SAFDNO against several neural operator baselines on a range of standard PDE benchmarks. 

5.1 Implementation Details 

All experiments are conducted using the AdamW optimizer for 500 training epochs with a learning rate of 1e − 3. The weight decay is set to 1e − 5 for the experiments in Table 1 and 1e − 4 for those in Table 2. For the learning rate schedule, we use the one-cycle policy for all experiments in Table 1 and cosine annealing for all experiments in Table 2. All experiments are performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. Detailed experiment setups are presented in Appendix D. 7Benchmarks and Baselines. We use several popular PDE benchmark datasets, including Darcy and Navier-Stokes [2, 32]; Pipe, Airfoil, and Plasticity in [8]. In addition, we also employ the Burgers equation, 1D Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), 2D CFD, and Reaction-Diffusion from PDEBench [33]. As the proposed SAFDNO is a spectral neural operator, we mainly compare SAFDNO with DeepONet [17], FNO [2], MWT [10], U-FNO [7], Geo-FNO [8], U-NO [34], F-FNO [20], LSM [35], and AM-FNO [4]. Additionally, we also consider several attention-based neural operators, including Galerkin [36], OFormer [18], GNOT [19], ONO [37], and Transolver [38].

## 6 Conclusion 

We presented SAFDNO, an adaptive spectral neural operator that replaces fixed truncated Fourier modes with the input-dependent TM system and amortized pole prediction. By combining SAFD-inspired adaptive representations with neural operator learning, SAFDNO improves the modeling of localized, nonstationary, and fine-scale PDE solution structures while retaining the global receptive field benefits of spectral operators. Empirically, SAFDNO achieves strong accuracy across diverse regular-grid PDE benchmarks and more robust zero-shot super-resolution performance on Darcy flow. Overall, our results highlight adaptive rational bases as a promising direction for operator learning.

Remark on the convergence rate. Regarding the convergence rate, we note that functional ap-proximation rate results are usually given in terms of the degree of smoothness of the function to be approximated: the higher the smoothness, the higher the convergence rate. For functions with analytic regularity, AFD, like other well-established approximation methods, has an exponential decay rate [13]. The convergence rate O(n−1/2) above is actually meant to be rather good, for it gives a borderline decay rate for the worst cases, including non-smooth boundary behavior and even discontinuities. As useful references, exact Shannon interpolation and the Karhunen–Loève expansion obey the same convergence rate. On the other hand, in practical applications, the residual energy of AFD is observed to decay rather fast. 28 D Detailed Experiment Setups
