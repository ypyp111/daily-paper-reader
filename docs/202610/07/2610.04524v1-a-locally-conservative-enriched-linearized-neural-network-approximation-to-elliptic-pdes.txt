A LOCALLY CONSERVATIVE ENRICHED LINEARIZED NEURAL NETWORK APPROXIMATION TO ELLIPTIC PDES ∗

SEUNGIL KIM † , GWANGHYUN JO ‡ , AND YOUNG JU LEE §

Abstract. This paper presents a locally conservative Enriched Linearized Neural Network (ENN) method for the Darcy flow model. A linearized shallow ReLU k network, whose hidden-layer parameters are fixed on a quasi-uniform set of the sphere, carries the approximation, and piecewise constant functions on an auxiliary subdivision of the domain are added to ensure local mass conservation. The subdivision may consist of general, possibly curved, elements. Since neural network functions do not satisfy the inverse inequality, the stability and error analysis of the standard discontinuous Galerkin interior penalty formulation do not apply. The missing inverse inequality is remedied by an edge identity combined with a Galerkin least-squares (GLS) term, together with a single neural approximant that is optimal in the L2, H1 and H2 norms simultaneously. We use the nonsymmetric interior penalty (NIPG) form, which is coercive for every positive penalty parameter. With n, the width of the shallow neural network, used in ENN, we prove an error estimate in the EG norm that is of the optimal H1 order, i.e., O(n−(r−1) /d ) for solutions in Hr , r ≥ 2, and O(n−(s−1) /d )for solutions in Hs, 32 < s ≤ 2, for any shape-regular subdivision, when the GLS parameter satisfies min {h, n −1/d }2 ≲ τ ≲ n−2/d and the edge terms are scaled with min {he, n −1/d }, i.e., by the resolution of the network rather than of the mesh. A numerical Darcy flux is constructed, which is locally conservative, regardless of the size or the number of the elements. Sample numerical results demonstrate the correctness of the theory. In particular, a fixed 4 × 4 subdivision gives the same accuracy as a subdivision refined with the network, and the mass loss stays at round-off unlike a non-conservative neural network method.

1. Introduction. Neural networks are attractive for solving partial differential equations (PDEs) because a network uθ : IR d → IR is a global, mesh-free function that can be evaluated anywhere, its derivatives are available exactly, and its approxi-mation power degrades with the dimension d far more slowly than that of grid-based methods, for which a tensor-product grid with M points per direction has M d points. These properties underlie the deep Ritz method [5] and the finite neuron method [26]. The main practical difficulties are the nonconvexity and ill-conditioning of training; linearized neural networks, random feature methods and extreme learning machines reduce them by fixing the hidden-layer parameters, so that the discrete problem be-comes linear. These methods, however, are not locally conservative. Conservation can be imposed as a hard constraint [11, 27]; here we obtain it from the approximation space instead. 2.2. Mesh and Piecewise Constant Functions. Conforming spaces, includ-ing neural networks, do not provide a conservative flux (see §5.3). The simplest remedy is to enrich the solution space by piecewise constant functions, so that conser-vation holds on each element of a mesh [7]. Strongly (pointwise) conservative neural networks have been devised [8], but they are expensive and not necessary for robust simulations, since local conservation is sufficient [7] in many practical applications. 6. Conclusion. We developed an enriched linearized neural network method for the Darcy problem, in which a fixed-feature ReLU k network ( k ≥ 2) carries the approximation and piecewise constants on an auxiliary subdivision provide local con-servation. Since neural functions have no inverse inequality, we used a Galerkin least-squares term together with an edge identity. This yields a C´ ea-type estimate with a constant independent of n, h, τ and α, and an error estimate in the EG norm of the optimal H1 order for every subdivision when min {h, n −1/d }2 ≲ τ ≲ n−2/d and the edge terms are scaled with min {he, n −1/d }, also for solutions in Hs with s > 32 . The numerical flux is locally conservative on every element of any subdivision. The nu-merical results confirm the predicted rates, also on a fixed 4 × 4 subdivision, and show mass conservation at round-off, in contrast to a non-conservative neural method. Fu-ture works include a proof of the boundedness of the neural trace constant, the IIPG and SIPG variants, an L2 error estimate, coefficients that are discontinuous inside elements, coupled flow and transport problems, and other more complicated PDEs.
